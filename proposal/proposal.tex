\begin{researchbox}[title={Executive Abstract}]
When a code-generating language model is trained with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning --- whether via teacher distillation, reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), or a hybrid two-stage pipeline --- does it acquire a \textbf{transferable algorithmic reasoning capability}, or does it merely learn to \textbf{recognize familiar problem contours and reduce them to memorized templates}?

This research proposal establishes a controlled, contamination-resistant evaluation suite and a prioritized multi-arm mitigation framework tailored for the programmatic code domain. We address two primary scientific dimensions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{The Diagnostic Reduction Ladder (L0--L5):} Unlike conventional rewriting benchmarks that mutate semantics while holding syntax static (e.g., Code-Rewriting / MRI~\cite{memorize_or_generalize}), our framework \textbf{holds underlying algorithmic semantics strictly invariant while systematically mutating surface form} (formatting, helper function abstraction, creative narrative reframing, and boundary constraints). Grounded in the peer-reviewed \textbf{EvoEval} (EMNLP 2024~\cite{evoeval}) suite, we pinpoint the exact \emph{collapse point} ($\ell^*$) and track multi-dimensional metrics: Pass@1/Pass@5 correctness, Token Efficiency (Per-Token Intelligence vs.\ Overthinking Tax~\cite{ockbench, thinking_longer}), Diagnostic Error Taxonomy (On-Path vs.\ Off-Path vs.\ Wrong-Template~\cite{depth_ceiling}), and Neutral Perturbation Consistency~\cite{flip_flop_consistency}.
  \item \textbf{The Prioritized Multi-Arm Mitigation Framework:} Rather than confining our exploration to a single technique, we formulate and empirically evaluate four prioritized mitigation arms across model capacities (SLMs $\sim$1.5B vs.\ Mid-tier $\sim$7B vs.\ Frontier LLMs):
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Arm 1 (Priority 1 --- Primary Mitigation):} Invariance-Regularized Policy Optimization (\textbf{Inv-GRPO}), employing paired cross-perturbation sampling and consistency rewards ($\mathcal{R}_{\text{consistency}}$) with zero inference latency overhead.
    \item \textbf{Arm 2 (Priority 2 --- SFT Distillation):} Contrastive Thought-Template SFT (\textbf{Contrastive-SFT}), fine-tuning on positive CoT paired with explicit negative template rejection traces~\cite{supercorrect, recode_contrastive}.
    \item \textbf{Arm 3 (Priority 3 --- Structural Syntax):} AST-Guided Policy Optimization (\textbf{AST-RL}) via tree-edit distance rewards (\texttt{simAST})~\cite{treediff, veriseek}.
    \item \textbf{Arm 4 (Priority 4 --- Process Verification):} Stepwise Execution-Gated RLVR (\textbf{Step-RLVR}) checking intermediate function contracts~\cite{codeprm, execverify}.
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

We provide a comprehensive 12-week (3-month) week-by-week execution timeline, a clean architectural software blueprint, and commodity edge hardware feasibility protocols (RTX 3070 8GB VRAM) directly targeting Orange Innovation telecommunications automation and self-healing infrastructure.
\end{researchbox}

\section{Core Research Question \& Paper Classification}
%============================================================

\subsection{1.1 The Core Question}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Central Thesis:} When a code-generating model is trained with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) --- via distillation, RL, or both --- does it learn a transferable reasoning skill, or does it learn to recognize familiar problem shapes and reduce them to templates it has already solved? How does this behavior systematically diverge between Small Language Models (SLMs $\sim$1--3B) and Large Language Models (LLMs)?
\end{keybox}

Capacity is expected to play a decisive structural role. Prior foundational literature~\cite{too_big_to_think} indicates that smaller models suffer from a tighter capacity bottleneck, forcing them to rely heavily on memorized pattern matching rather than latent planning. This project constructs an empirical benchmark to quantify this boundary and proposes targeted training interventions to push the reasoning ceiling outward on edge-sized models.

\subsection{1.2 Paper Classification \& Scope}

This research is structured in two complementary tiers:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Primary Tier --- Benchmark \& Evaluation Methodology Paper (Empirical):} A contamination-controlled evaluation suite applied across open-source and frontier models of varying parameter sizes, reporting and analyzing degradation curves, token efficiency, and error taxonomies.
  \item \textbf{Extended Tier --- Diagnose-and-Improve Intervention Paper (Methodological):} Applying targeted SFT and RL interventions (Distillation, Vanilla RLVR, AST-RL, and novel Inv-GRPO) to demonstrate that the diagnosed collapse point can be actively shifted to harder ladder levels on resource-constrained SLMs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Foundational Rationale: Why the Code Domain?}
%============================================================

A critical design choice of this research is its deliberate focus on \textbf{programmatic code generation} rather than generic natural language reasoning or mathematical word problems.

\begin{domainbox}[title={\faCode\ Foundational Rationale: Why Code is the Optimal Scientific Laboratory}]
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Deterministic, Objective Ground-Truth Verification (Zero LLM-Judge Bias):}
  In open-ended NLP or multi-step qualitative reasoning, evaluating intermediate derivations requires subjective human grading or error-prone LLM-as-a-judge heuristics. In contrast, code execution yields binary, deterministic, and sandbox-verifiable ground truth ($0/1$ pass/fail against unit tests) with microsecond precision.
  
  \item \textbf{Strict Semantic Equivalence vs.\ Surface Syntax (AST Isomorphism):}
  In natural language, paraphrasing alters meaning subtly. In code, modifying variable names (\texttt{nums} $\to$ \texttt{alpha\_seq}), reordering independent statements, or introducing helper functions preserves the \textbf{exact operational semantics and Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) invariants} while completely transforming the surface token distribution. This provides a clean mathematical baseline to isolate reasoning from memorization.
  
  \item \textbf{Combinatorial State Space \& Edge Case Fragility:}
  Code synthesis requires multi-step state tracking, boundary condition handling, and algorithmic invariant maintenance. A model relying on shortcut pattern matching will fail catastrophically on edge cases, making shortcut learning immediately observable and measurable.
  
  \item \textbf{Direct Enterprise Impact on Orange Product Stack:}
  Validating and hardening code SLMs provides direct deployment value to Orange Innovation's network automation, self-healing systems, and internal developer tools.
\end{enumerate}
\end{domainbox}

\section{Comprehensive Literature Review \& Theoretical Foundations}
%============================================================

Our theoretical foundation synthesizes five complementary bodies of literature:

\subsection{3.1 The Foundational Six Papers: Limits of Reasoning}

Our methodology directly builds upon six foundational papers that formalized reasoning limits, planning depth, and memorization trade-offs:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=6pt]
  \item \textbf{P1 --- OOD Generalization of Reasoning in Multimodal LLMs for Visual Planning~\cite{ood_visual_planning}:} Proved that Chain-of-Thought (CoT) helps primarily within-distribution, while performance collapses abruptly under slight out-of-distribution domain shifts, confirming that models learn the ``shape'' of the reasoning trace rather than absolute logic.
  \item \textbf{P2 --- Does RL Really Incentivize Reasoning Capacity Beyond the Base Model?~\cite{rlvr_limits}:} Established the pass@1 vs.\ pass@$k$ exploration methodology; demonstrated that RLVR primarily elevates sampling efficiency for solutions already reachable in the base model's distribution, whereas knowledge distillation is the primary mechanism that introduces genuinely novel capability.
  \item \textbf{P3 --- The Depth Ceiling: Limits of LLMs in Discovering Latent Planning~\cite{depth_ceiling}:} Discovered a hard cap on latent (hidden) planning depth (3--7 steps depending on parameter count), showing that explicit CoT lifts the cap to $\sim$20 steps; introduced the \textbf{on-path vs.\ off-path} error distinction which we adapt for our code error taxonomy.
  \item \textbf{P4 --- Trapped in the Past? Fluid vs.\ Crystallized Intelligence via Chess~\cite{trapped_in_past}:} Provided a methodological template for constructing difficulty taxonomies by distance from training distribution without requiring access to proprietary pre-training corpora.
  \item \textbf{P5 --- Too Big to Think: Capacity, Memorization, and Generalization~\cite{too_big_to_think}:} Formalized the structural trade-off between model parameter capacity and memorization vs.\ generalization, proving that lower-capacity models are structurally biased towards memorizing shortcut templates rather than learning generalized reasoning --- directly motivating our SLM vs.\ LLM capacity axis.
  \item \textbf{P6 --- Beyond Memorization: Reductive vs.\ Epistemic Reasoning via Logic Puzzles~\cite{beyond_memorization}:} Introduced \textbf{reduction} as the unifying theoretical concept (memorization being the limiting case of reduction) and formulated the reduction-ladder methodology which we adapt to the code domain.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{3.2 Code-Domain Memorization \& Contamination Literature}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{EvoEval (EMNLP 2024)~\cite{evoeval}:} Created five semantic perturbation dimensions for HumanEval, establishing that 57 state-of-the-art LLMs suffer an average 38--40\% degradation across subtle, creative, and combined transformations.
  \item \textbf{Memorize or Generalize? Evaluating LLM Code Generation with Code-Rewriting~\cite{memorize_or_generalize}:} Introduced code-rewriting and the Memorization Risk Index (MRI). \emph{Key Contrast:} They mutate semantics while preserving surface syntax, whereas our Reduction Ladder \textbf{preserves underlying semantics while systematically mutating surface form and context}.
  \item \textbf{LLM Performance for Code Generation on Noisy Tasks~\cite{noisy_code_tasks}:} Identified ``eager pattern matching'' and proposed performance decay under obfuscation as an empirical contamination detector.
  \item \textbf{LiveCodeBench~\cite{livecodebench}:} Continuously harvests contamination-free problems from LeetCode, AtCoder, and Codeforces post-cutoff, serving as our clean temporal control set.
  \item \textbf{LeetCodeDataset~\cite{leetcode_dataset}:} Provided temporal pre/post-2024 splits and rich test cases, serving as our curated seed problem source.
  \item \textbf{DynaCode~\cite{dynacode}:} Dynamically perturbs MBPP/MBPP+ problems, demonstrating sharp degradation in static benchmarks due to data contamination.
  \item \textbf{Learned or Memorized? Quantifying Memorization Advantage in Code LLMs~\cite{learned_or_memorized}:} Introduced perturbation-based memorization advantage scores across 8 open-source code models and 19 benchmarks.
  \item \textbf{CRUXEval (ICML 2024)~\cite{cruxeval}:} Evaluated code reasoning via input/output execution prediction, proving that code understanding lags far behind code generation.
  \item \textbf{GSM-Symbolic (ICLR 2025)~\cite{gsm_symbolic}:} Demonstrated that non-functional prompt variations induce catastrophic reasoning collapse in mathematical models.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{3.3 Multi-Dimensional Evaluation Dimensions Beyond Correctness}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{OckBench --- Per-Token Intelligence~\cite{ockbench}:} Showed that larger models reach correct answers with denser, shorter reasoning, while smaller models pay an ``Overthinking Tax'' on complex tasks.
  \item \textbf{Thinking Longer, Not Always Smarter~\cite{thinking_longer}:} Empirically proved that models generate significantly more reasoning tokens on incorrect attempts than on successful ones (up to 45\% token overhead for failure).
  \item \textbf{Attribution Graphs \& Faithfulness~\cite{faithfulness_graphs}:} Evaluated whether generated reasoning traces are genuinely causal via step-deletion perturbations.
  \item \textbf{Flip-Flop Consistency~\cite{flip_flop_consistency}:} Documented substantial accuracy swings resulting purely from neutral prompt changes (variable renaming, casing, argument ordering).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{4.1 Inverse Transformation: Reduction Ladder vs.\ Code-Rewriting (MRI)}

\begin{researchbox}[title={Theoretical Contrast: Reduction Ladder vs.\ Code-Rewriting (MRI)}]
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Code-Rewriting (MRI Approach~\cite{memorize_or_generalize}):} \emph{Varies semantics while holding surface syntax fixed.} Tests whether a model blindly generates familiar code when the underlying mathematical requirements have changed.
  \item \textbf{Reduction Ladder (Our Approach):} \emph{Holds underlying algorithmic semantics strictly invariant while systematically mutating surface form and context.} Tests whether a model fails to apply a valid algorithmic solution merely because the surface tokens and problem framing have shifted.
\end{itemize}
\end{researchbox}

\subsection{4.2 Concrete Transformation Walkthrough: Two Sum (L0--L5)}

\begin{researchbox}[title={Concrete Transformation Walkthrough: Two Sum Across L0--L5}]
\textbf{L0 (Verbatim Baseline):} Given an array of integers \texttt{nums} and integer \texttt{target}, return indices of the two numbers such that they add up to \texttt{target}.\\[4pt]
\textbf{L1 (Format / Subtle):} Given a comma-delimited string \texttt{"2,7,11,15"} and integer target 9, parse values and return the 0-indexed integer indices of the pair.\\[4pt]
\textbf{L2 (ToolUse / Structural):} Implement \texttt{find\_pair(seq, target)} using a pre-defined helper function \texttt{hash\_lookup(table, key)} to manage lookup operations.\\[4pt]
\textbf{L3 (Creative Context):} In an Orange cloud server pool, $n$ containers require bandwidth units $b_1 \ldots b_n$. Find two containers whose combined bandwidth exactly matches the gateway cap $B$.\\[4pt]
\textbf{L4 (Constraint Added):} Same as L3, but the two containers must belong to \emph{different availability zones} (requires tracking secondary categorical attributes).\\[4pt]
\textbf{L5 (Concept Combination):} Given task bandwidths and execution windows, find two concurrent tasks that sum to capacity while \emph{minimizing total scheduling fragmentation}.
\end{researchbox}

\section{Multi-Dimensional Evaluation Methodology}
%============================================================

Beyond standard binary correctness, our evaluation harness captures four orthogonal dimensions:

\subsection{5.1 Correctness (Base Metric)}
Pass/fail status against held-out unit tests executed in an isolated, timeout-guarded multiprocess sandbox.
\[
\text{pass@1} = \frac{1}{|D|} \sum_{i=1}^{|D|} \mathbb{I}(\text{sample}_1 == \text{PASS}), \quad \text{pass@5} = 1 - \frac{\binom{n-c}{5}}{\binom{n}{5}}
\]

\subsection{5.2 Token Efficiency \& Compute Density}
Tracking reasoning-trace token length ($\tau_\ell$) across ladder levels.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Hypothesis:} Genuine reasoning exhibits token usage that scales adaptively with structural problem complexity (L4/L5). Pattern matching displays either flat token usage (unconditional template dump) or an unproductive ``Overthinking Tax'' (excess tokens with zero accuracy gain~\cite{ockbench, thinking_longer}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{5.3 Diagnostic Error Taxonomy}
When an attempt fails, it is categorized into:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{On-Path Failure:} Correct algorithmic approach, flawed execution (e.g., off-by-one index, boundary inequality).
  \item \textbf{Off-Path Failure:} Algorithmic logic lost entirely; hallucinated control flow.
  \item \textbf{Wrong-Template Failure:} Confidently applied a memorized template from an unrelated classic problem. \emph{Category (3) provides the strongest direct proof of pattern-matching collapse.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{5.4 Consistency Under Neutral Perturbation ($\Delta_c$)}
Re-running identical logical problems with purely neutral surface variations (variable names, argument ordering). Any accuracy drop with zero semantic change confirms surface token dependency:
\[
\Delta_c = |\text{pass@1}(L_1) - \text{pass@1}(L_2)|
\]

\subsection{5.5 Collapse Point ($\ell^*$) \& Ladder AUC ($\mathcal{A}$)}
\[
\ell^* = \min \left\{ \ell \in \{0,1,2,3,4,5\} \mid \text{pass@1}(\ell) < 0.50 \right\}, \quad \mathcal{A} = \frac{1}{6} \sum_{\ell=0}^{5} \text{pass@1}(\ell)
\]

\section{Model Selection Rationale \& Capacity Axis}
%============================================================

A critical question for any benchmark-driven research is: \emph{why this specific model family?} Our selection of \texttt{Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct} as the primary experimental target is deliberate, scientifically motivated, and aligned with the broader research community.

\subsection{6.1 Why Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct?}

\begin{domainbox}[title={\faFlask\ Scientific Rationale for Primary Model Selection}]
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=6pt]
  \item \textbf{State-of-the-Art in the Sub-2B Code SLM Class.}
  At the time of writing, \texttt{Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct} holds the highest Pass@1 scores among all publicly available code-generating models with fewer than 2 billion parameters. It consistently outperforms \texttt{DeepSeek-Coder-1.3B}, \texttt{StarCoder2-3B}, and even the legacy \texttt{CodeLlama-7B} on standard benchmarks (HumanEval, MBPP, MultiPL-E). This ensures we are testing against the \emph{strongest possible baseline} rather than an artificially weak one.

  \item \textbf{The De-Facto Community Standard for Reasoning Research.}
  The DeepSeek-R1 project (2025) --- the most influential open-source reasoning initiative to date --- selected the Qwen-1.5B architecture as its \emph{first and primary distillation target}, releasing \texttt{DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B} as the reference checkpoint for small-model reasoning research. This established the Qwen-1.5B family as the \textbf{de-facto community standard} for RLVR and distillation studies, ensuring our results are directly comparable to the rapidly growing body of literature built atop this foundation.

  \item \textbf{The Ideal ``Capacity Bottleneck'' Laboratory.}
  Paper~5~\cite{too_big_to_think} formalized the theoretical trade-off between model capacity and the memorization--generalization frontier. Larger models ($>$30B parameters) can mask shortcut learning through brute-force memorization of millions of surface patterns. A 1.5B-parameter model operates under a severe \textbf{capacity bottleneck} that structurally forces reliance on compressed templates, making shortcut collapse \emph{observable and measurable}. If our mitigation (Inv-GRPO) can demonstrably shift the collapse point ($\ell^*$) rightward on such a constrained model, this constitutes strong evidence that the intervention induces genuine algorithmic transfer rather than expanded memorization.

  \item \textbf{Edge Deployment Feasibility for Orange Infrastructure.}
  At 4-bit NF4 precision, the model consumes only $\sim$1.1~GB of VRAM, fitting comfortably within a single NVIDIA RTX~3070 (8~GB). This directly validates deployment viability on Orange Innovation's edge servers, telecom base stations, and on-device automation agents --- a core deliverable of this research.
\end{enumerate}
\end{domainbox}

\subsection{7.2 Direct Reproduction \& Extension of EvoEval Baselines}

EvoEval~\cite{evoeval} reported degradation profiles for 57~models across five perturbation types. Since our Reduction Ladder levels L1--L5 are built directly from the EvoEval dataset suite, we can perform \textbf{exact apple-to-apple comparisons}:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{Baseline Reproduction:} We first reproduce the EvoEval-reported Pass@1 for \texttt{Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B} and \texttt{7B} on the same five splits (Subtle, ToolUse, Creative, Difficult, Combine) to verify experimental alignment.
  \item \textbf{Mitigation Delta ($\Delta_{\text{mit}}$):} For each mitigation arm (M2--M6), we report the \emph{absolute improvement} over both (a)~our own M1 baseline and (b)~the EvoEval-reported baseline for the same model family:
  \[
  \Delta_{\text{mit}}(\ell) = \text{Pass@1}_{\text{M}_k}(\ell) - \text{Pass@1}_{\text{M}_1}(\ell)
  \]
  \item \textbf{Collapse Point Shift ($\Delta \ell^*$):} The primary success metric --- how many ladder levels the collapse point is delayed:
  \[
  \Delta \ell^* = \ell^*_{\text{M}_k} - \ell^*_{\text{M}_1}
  \]
  A shift of $\Delta \ell^* \geq 2$ (e.g., from L2 to L4) constitutes a strong positive result.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{7.4 Statistical Rigor \& Reporting Standards}

To ensure our comparisons are scientifically defensible:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{Deterministic Reproducibility:} All Pass@1 evaluations use greedy decoding ($T=0.0$), ensuring bit-exact reproducibility across runs.
  \item \textbf{Unbiased Pass@5:} Following the Chen et al.\ (2021) unbiased estimator with $n=20$ samples at $T=0.8$.
  \item \textbf{Effect Size Reporting:} Beyond raw accuracy, we report $\Delta_{\text{mit}}$ (absolute improvement), $\Delta\ell^*$ (collapse shift), and relative MRI reduction (\%) for each mitigation arm.
  \item \textbf{Ablation Controls:} Each mitigation arm is evaluated independently against the shared M1 baseline, preventing confounding between interventions.
  \item \textbf{Contamination Guard:} The LiveCodeBench control set provides a temporal firewall; any model whose LiveCodeBench score deviates anomalously from its ladder profile triggers a contamination investigation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{8.1 Arm 1 (Priority 1 --- Primary): Invariance-Regularized GRPO (Inv-GRPO)}

During policy rollouts, Inv-GRPO feeds paired semantically equivalent prompts $(x, x')$:
\[
\mathcal{R}_{\text{total}}(y_i, y'_i) = \mathcal{R}_{\text{exec}}(y_i) + \mathcal{R}_{\text{exec}}(y'_i) + \lambda \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\text{consistency}}(y_i, y'_i) - \gamma \cdot \mathcal{P}_{\text{template}}
\]
where $\mathcal{R}_{\text{consistency}} = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{R}_{\text{exec}}(y_i) = 1 \land \mathcal{R}_{\text{exec}}(y'_i) = 1)$ penalizes contradictory outputs with zero inference latency overhead.

\subsection{8.2 Arm 2 (Priority 2): Contrastive Thought-Template SFT}

Inspired by SuperCorrect~\cite{supercorrect}, this arm augments SFT training data with negative shortcut examples:
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{Contrastive}} = -\sum_{t=1}^{T} \log P_\theta(y_t^+ \mid x, y_{<t}^+) + \alpha \max \left( 0, \log P_\theta(y^- \mid x) - \log P_\theta(y^+ \mid x) + m \right)
\]

\subsection{8.3 Arm 3 (Priority 3): AST-Guided Structural Policy Optimization (AST-RL)}

This arm integrates a deterministic syntax parser into the RL reward loop~\cite{treediff, veriseek}:
\[
\mathcal{R}_{\text{AST}}(y) = \exp \left( - \alpha \cdot \text{TreeDist}(\text{AST}(y), \text{AST}(y^*)) \right)
\]

\subsection{8.4 Arm 4 (Priority 4): Stepwise Execution-Gated RLVR (Step-RLVR)}

Building upon CodePRM~\cite{codeprm} and ExecVerify~\cite{execverify}, this arm checks pre- and post-conditions of sub-functions during sandbox execution, awarding partial reward credits for correct algorithmic sub-goals in complex L4/L5 problems.

\section{Conclusion}
%============================================================

By holding algorithmic semantics strictly invariant while perturbing surface framing, the \textbf{Reduction Ladder} provides a contamination-resistant diagnostic for code-generating models. Our deliberate selection of \texttt{Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct} --- the de-facto community standard for small-model reasoning research --- ensures that our results are directly comparable to the rapidly growing body of RLVR and distillation literature. Coupling this diagnostic with our \textbf{Prioritized Multi-Arm Mitigation Framework} (specifically Inv-GRPO) and a rigorous five-axis cross-paper comparison methodology offers a clear, reproducible pathway to breaking the memorisation barrier in edge-deployed SLMs.
