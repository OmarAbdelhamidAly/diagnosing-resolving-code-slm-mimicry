\begin{researchbox}[title={Executive Abstract}]
When a code-generating language model is trained with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning --- whether via teacher distillation, reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), or a hybrid two-stage pipeline --- does it acquire a \textbf{transferable algorithmic reasoning capability}, or does it merely learn to \textbf{recognize familiar problem contours and reduce them to memorized templates}?

This research proposal establishes a controlled, contamination-resistant evaluation suite and mitigation framework specifically tailored for the programmatic code domain. We address two primary scientific dimensions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{The Diagnostic Reduction Ladder (L0--L5):} Unlike conventional rewriting benchmarks that mutate semantics while holding syntax static (e.g., Code-Rewriting / MRI), our framework \textbf{holds underlying algorithmic semantics strictly invariant while systematically mutating surface form} (formatting, identifier naming, helper function abstraction, creative narrative reframing, and boundary constraints). Grounded in the peer-reviewed \textbf{EvoEval} (EMNLP 2024) suite, we pinpoint the exact \emph{collapse point} ($\ell^*$) and track multi-dimensional metrics: Pass@1/Pass@5 correctness, Token Efficiency (Per-Token Intelligence vs.\ Overthinking Tax), Diagnostic Error Taxonomy (On-Path vs.\ Off-Path vs.\ Wrong-Template), and Neutral Perturbation Consistency.
  \item \textbf{The Diagnose-and-Improve Intervention Framework:} We extend pure evaluation into an actionable empirical comparison across model capacities (SLMs $\sim$1.5B vs.\ Mid-tier $\sim$7B vs.\ Frontier LLMs) and prioritize four mitigation arms:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Arm 1 (Priority 1 --- Primary):} Invariance-Regularized Policy Optimization (\textbf{Inv-GRPO}), employing paired cross-perturbation sampling and consistency rewards ($\mathcal{R}_{\text{consistency}}$) with zero inference latency overhead.
    \item \textbf{Arm 2 (Priority 2 --- Distillation):} Contrastive Thought-Template SFT (\textbf{Contrastive-SFT}), fine-tuning on positive CoT paired with explicit negative template rejection traces.
    \item \textbf{Arm 3 (Priority 3 --- Syntax):} AST-Guided Policy Optimization (\textbf{AST-RL}) via tree-edit distance rewards (\texttt{simAST}).
    \item \textbf{Arm 4 (Priority 4 --- Process):} Stepwise Execution-Gated RLVR (\textbf{Step-RLVR}) checking intermediate function contracts.
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

We provide a comprehensive 12-week (3-month) execution timeline, a clean architectural software blueprint, and commodity edge hardware feasibility protocols (RTX 3070 8GB VRAM) directly targeting Orange Innovation telecommunications automation and self-healing infrastructure.
\end{researchbox}

\section{Core Research Question \& Paper Classification}
%============================================================

\subsection{1.1 The Core Question}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Central Thesis:} When a code-generating model is trained with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) --- via distillation, RL, or both --- does it learn a transferable reasoning skill, or does it learn to recognize familiar problem shapes and reduce them to templates it has already solved? How does this behavior systematically diverge between Small Language Models (SLMs $\sim$1--3B) and Large Language Models (LLMs)?
\end{keybox}

Capacity is expected to play a decisive structural role. Prior foundational literature indicates that smaller models suffer from a tighter capacity bottleneck, forcing them to rely heavily on memorized pattern matching rather than latent planning. This project constructs an empirical benchmark to quantify this boundary and proposes targeted training interventions to push the reasoning ceiling outward on edge-sized models.

\subsection{1.2 Paper Classification \& Scope}

This research is structured in two complementary tiers:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Primary Tier --- Benchmark \& Evaluation Methodology Paper (Empirical):} A contamination-controlled evaluation suite applied across open-source and frontier models of varying parameter sizes, reporting and analyzing degradation curves, token efficiency, and error taxonomies.
  \item \textbf{Extended Tier --- Diagnose-and-Improve Intervention Paper (Methodological):} Applying targeted SFT and RL interventions (Distillation, Vanilla RLVR, and novel Inv-GRPO) to demonstrate that the diagnosed collapse point can be actively shifted to harder ladder levels on resource-constrained SLMs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Foundational Rationale: Why the Code Domain?}
%============================================================

A critical design choice of this research is its deliberate focus on \textbf{programmatic code generation} rather than generic natural language reasoning or mathematical word problems.

\begin{domainbox}[title={\faCode\ Foundational Rationale: Why Code is the Optimal Scientific Laboratory}]
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Deterministic, Objective Ground-Truth Verification (Zero LLM-Judge Bias):}
  In open-ended NLP or multi-step qualitative reasoning, evaluating intermediate derivations requires subjective human grading or error-prone LLM-as-a-judge heuristics. In contrast, code execution yields binary, deterministic, and sandbox-verifiable ground truth ($0/1$ pass/fail against unit tests) with microsecond precision.
  
  \item \textbf{Strict Semantic Equivalence vs.\ Surface Syntax (AST Isomorphism):}
  In natural language, paraphrasing alters meaning subtly. In code, modifying variable names (\texttt{nums} $\to$ \texttt{alpha\_seq}), reordering independent statements, or introducing helper functions preserves the \textbf{exact operational semantics and Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) invariants} while completely transforming the surface token distribution. This provides a clean mathematical baseline to isolate reasoning from memorization.
  
  \item \textbf{Combinatorial State Space \& Edge Case Fragility:}
  Code synthesis requires multi-step state tracking, boundary condition handling, and algorithmic invariant maintenance. A model relying on shortcut pattern matching will fail catastrophically on edge cases, making shortcut learning immediately observable and measurable.
  
  \item \textbf{Direct Enterprise Impact on Orange Product Stack:}
  Validating and hardening code SLMs provides direct deployment value to Orange Innovation's network automation, self-healing systems, and internal developer tools.
\end{enumerate}
\end{domainbox}

\section{Comprehensive Literature Review \& Theoretical Foundations}
%============================================================

Our theoretical foundation synthesizes four complementary bodies of literature:

\subsection{3.1 The Foundational Six Papers}

Our methodology directly builds upon six foundational papers that formalized reasoning limits, planning depth, and memorization trade-offs:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{P1 --- OOD Generalization of Reasoning in Multimodal LLMs for Visual Planning~\cite{ood_visual_planning}:} Proved that Chain-of-Thought (CoT) helps primarily within-distribution, while performance collapses abruptly under slight out-of-distribution domain shifts.
  \item \textbf{P2 --- Does RL Really Incentivize Reasoning Capacity Beyond the Base Model?~\cite{rlvr_limits}:} Established the pass@1 vs.\ pass@$k$ exploration methodology; demonstrated that RLVR primarily elevates sampling efficiency for solutions already reachable in the base model's distribution, whereas knowledge distillation is the primary mechanism that introduces genuinely novel capability.
  \item \textbf{P3 --- The Depth Ceiling: Limits of LLMs in Discovering Latent Planning~\cite{depth_ceiling}:} Discovered a hard cap on latent (hidden) planning depth (3--7 steps depending on parameter count), showing that explicit CoT lifts the cap to $\sim$20 steps; introduced the \textbf{on-path vs.\ off-path} error distinction which we adapt for our code error taxonomy.
  \item \textbf{P4 --- Trapped in the Past? Fluid vs.\ Crystallized Intelligence via Chess~\cite{trapped_in_past}:} Provided a methodological template for constructing difficulty taxonomies by distance from training distribution without requiring access to proprietary pre-training corpora.
  \item \textbf{P5 --- Too Big to Think: Capacity, Memorization, and Generalization~\cite{too_big_to_think}:} Formalized the structural trade-off between model parameter capacity and memorization vs.\ generalization, proving that jointly training on both never yields extrapolation regardless of size --- directly motivating our SLM vs.\ LLM capacity axis.
  \item \textbf{P6 --- Beyond Memorization: Reductive vs.\ Epistemic Reasoning via Logic Puzzles~\cite{beyond_memorization}:} Introduced \textbf{reduction} as the unifying theoretical concept (memorization being the limiting case of reduction) and formulated the reduction-ladder methodology which we adapt to the code domain.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{3.2 Code-Domain Memorization \& Contamination Literature}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Memorize or Generalize? Evaluating LLM Code Generation with Code-Rewriting~\cite{memorize_or_generalize}:} Introduced code-rewriting and the Memorization Risk Index (MRI). \emph{Key Relationship:} This is the closest prior work to our proposal, but performs the \textbf{inverse transformation} of what we propose: they mutate semantics while preserving surface syntax, whereas our Reduction Ladder \textbf{preserves underlying semantics while systematically mutating surface form}.
  \item \textbf{LLM Performance for Code Generation on Noisy Tasks~\cite{noisy_code_tasks}:} Identified ``eager pattern matching'' and proposed performance decay under obfuscation as an empirical contamination detector.
  \item \textbf{LiveCodeBench~\cite{livecodebench}:} Continuously harvests contamination-free problems from LeetCode, AtCoder, and Codeforces post-cutoff, serving as our clean temporal control set.
  \item \textbf{LeetCodeDataset~\cite{leetcode_dataset}:} Provided temporal pre/post-2024 splits and rich test cases, serving as our curated seed problem source.
  \item \textbf{DynaCode~\cite{dynacode}:} Dynamically perturbs MBPP/MBPP+ problems, demonstrating sharp degradation in static benchmarks due to data contamination.
  \item \textbf{Learned or Memorized? Quantifying Memorization Advantage in Code LLMs~\cite{learned_or_memorized}:} Introduced perturbation-based memorization advantage scores across 8 open-source code models and 19 benchmarks.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{3.3 Multi-Dimensional Evaluation Dimensions Beyond Correctness}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{OckBench --- Per-Token Intelligence \& Overthinking Tax~\cite{ockbench}:} Showed that larger models reach correct answers with denser, shorter reasoning, while smaller models pay an ``Overthinking Tax'' on complex tasks.
  \item \textbf{Thinking Longer, Not Always Smarter~\cite{thinking_longer}:} Empirically proved that models generate significantly more reasoning tokens on incorrect attempts than on successful ones (up to 45\% token overhead for failure).
  \item \textbf{Attribution Graphs \& Faithfulness~\cite{faithfulness_graphs}:} Evaluated whether generated reasoning traces are genuinely causal via step-deletion perturbations.
  \item \textbf{Flip-Flop Consistency~\cite{flip_flop_consistency}:} Documented substantial accuracy swings resulting purely from neutral prompt changes (variable renaming, casing, argument ordering).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{4.1 Inverse Transformation: Reduction Ladder vs.\ Code-Rewriting (MRI)}

\begin{researchbox}[title={Theoretical Contrast: Reduction Ladder vs.\ Code-Rewriting (MRI)}]
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Code-Rewriting (MRI Approach~\cite{memorize_or_generalize}):} \emph{Varies semantics while holding surface syntax fixed.} Tests whether a model blindly generates familiar code when the underlying mathematical requirements have changed.
  \item \textbf{Reduction Ladder (Our Approach):} \emph{Holds underlying algorithmic semantics strictly invariant while systematically mutating surface form and context.} Tests whether a model fails to apply a valid algorithmic solution merely because the surface tokens and problem framing have shifted.
\end{itemize}
\end{researchbox}

\subsection{4.2 Concrete Transformation Walkthrough: Two Sum (L0--L5)}

\begin{researchbox}[title={Concrete Transformation Walkthrough: Two Sum Across L0--L5}]
\textbf{L0 (Verbatim Baseline):} Given an array of integers \texttt{nums} and integer \texttt{target}, return indices of the two numbers such that they add up to \texttt{target}.\\[4pt]
\textbf{L1 (Format / Subtle):} Given a comma-delimited string \texttt{"2,7,11,15"} and integer target 9, parse values and return the 0-indexed integer indices of the pair.\\[4pt]
\textbf{L2 (ToolUse / Structural):} Implement \texttt{find\_pair(seq, target)} using a pre-defined helper function \texttt{hash\_lookup(table, key)} to manage lookup operations.\\[4pt]
\textbf{L3 (Creative Context):} In an Orange cloud server pool, $n$ containers require bandwidth units $b_1 \ldots b_n$. Find two containers whose combined bandwidth exactly matches the gateway cap $B$.\\[4pt]
\textbf{L4 (Constraint Added):} Same as L3, but the two containers must belong to \emph{different availability zones} (requires tracking secondary categorical attributes).\\[4pt]
\textbf{L5 (Concept Combination):} Given task bandwidths and execution windows, find two concurrent tasks that sum to capacity while \emph{minimizing total scheduling fragmentation}.
\end{researchbox}

\section{Multi-Dimensional Evaluation Methodology}
%============================================================

Beyond standard binary correctness, our evaluation harness captures four orthogonal dimensions:

\subsection{5.1 Correctness (Base Metric)}
Pass/fail status against held-out unit tests executed in an isolated, timeout-guarded multiprocess sandbox.
\[
\text{pass@1} = \frac{1}{|D|} \sum_{i=1}^{|D|} \mathbb{I}(\text{sample}_1 == \text{PASS}), \quad \text{pass@5} = 1 - \frac{\binom{n-c}{5}}{\binom{n}{5}}
\]

\subsection{5.2 Token Efficiency \& Compute Density}
Tracking reasoning-trace token length ($\tau_\ell$) across ladder levels.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Hypothesis:} Genuine reasoning exhibits token usage that scales adaptively with structural problem complexity (L4/L5). Pattern matching displays either flat token usage (unconditional template dump) or an unproductive ``Overthinking Tax'' (excess tokens with zero accuracy gain~\cite{ockbench, thinking_longer}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{5.3 Diagnostic Error Taxonomy}
When an attempt fails, it is categorized into:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{On-Path Failure:} Correct algorithmic approach, flawed execution (e.g., off-by-one index, boundary inequality).
  \item \textbf{Off-Path Failure:} Algorithmic logic lost entirely; hallucinated control flow.
  \item \textbf{Wrong-Template Failure:} Confidently applied a memorized template from an unrelated classic problem. \emph{Category (3) provides the strongest direct proof of pattern-matching collapse.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{5.4 Consistency Under Neutral Perturbation ($\Delta_c$)}
Re-running identical logical problems with purely neutral surface variations (variable names, argument ordering). Any accuracy drop with zero semantic change confirms surface token dependency:
\[
\Delta_c = |\text{pass@1}(L_1) - \text{pass@1}(L_2)|
\]

\subsection{5.5 Collapse Point ($\ell^*$) \& Ladder AUC ($\mathcal{A}$)}
\[
\ell^* = \min \left\{ \ell \in \{0,1,2,3,4,5\} \mid \text{pass@1}(\ell) < 0.50 \right\}, \quad \mathcal{A} = \frac{1}{6} \sum_{\ell=0}^{5} \text{pass@1}(\ell)
\]

\subsection{7.2 Combined Strategy: Distillation Followed by RLVR (DeepSeek-R1 Pattern)}

If an SLM does not possess a capability within its sampling distribution, standalone RLVR stalls under the \textbf{sparse-reward problem}. Running Distillation first seeds the reasoning distribution; subsequent RLVR then optimizes and hardens this capability against reward hacking.

\subsection{7.3 Proposed Novel Method: Invariance-Regularized GRPO (Inv-GRPO)}

During policy rollouts, Inv-GRPO feeds paired semantically equivalent prompts $(x, x')$:
\[
\mathcal{R}_{\text{total}}(y_i, y'_i) = \mathcal{R}_{\text{exec}}(y_i) + \mathcal{R}_{\text{exec}}(y'_i) + \lambda \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\text{consistency}}(y_i, y'_i) - \gamma \cdot \mathcal{P}_{\text{template}}
\]
where $\mathcal{R}_{\text{consistency}} = \mathbb{I}(\mathcal{R}_{\text{exec}}(y_i) = 1 \land \mathcal{R}_{\text{exec}}(y'_i) = 1)$ penalizes contradictory outputs with zero inference latency overhead. Group advantage is computed over paired completions:
\[
\hat{A}_i = \frac{\mathcal{R}_{\text{total}}(y_i, y'_i) - \mu_{\mathcal{R}}}{\sigma_{\mathcal{R}} + \epsilon}
\]

\midrule
\textbf{Chain-of-Thought (CoT)} & Writing out step-by-step intermediate reasoning derivations before generating the final code solution. \\
\textbf{RLVR} & Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards; training policy using objective automated unit-test pass/fail signals ($0/1$). \\
\textbf{RLIR} & Reinforcement Learning with Intrinsic Rewards; self-rewarding RL where the model evaluates its own reasoning (deprioritized due to reward hacking). \\

\textbf{Sparse Reward Trap} & Training failure occurring when an RL policy rarely succeeds on its own, leaving the optimizer without positive gradient signal. \\
\textbf{Reward Hacking} & A model finding superficial shortcuts that pass unit tests without synthesizing genuine invariant algorithmic logic. \\
\textbf{LoRA / QLoRA} & Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning updating low-rank adapter matrices ($r=16$) over 4-bit quantized base weights on a single GPU. \\
\textbf{Diagnose-and-Improve} & An empirical research methodology that first pinpoints failure modes via diagnostic probing, applies targeted training interventions, and proves collapse point delay. \\
\textbf{Per-Token Intelligence} & Metric measuring reasoning density: ratio of accuracy achieved per generated reasoning token~\cite{ockbench}. \\
\textbf{Overthinking Tax} & Excessive generation of redundant reasoning tokens that fails to improve final execution accuracy~\cite{thinking_longer}. \\
